The Origin

Three decades of chemicals.
Six years to undo them.

The land ANAAD farms today had been under conventional cultivation since the 1980s — compacted by machinery, dependent on synthetic nitrogen, stripped of its biological diversity. The restoration did not begin with a new input. It began with a decision to stop.

We stopped the chemicals. We stopped the hybrids. We stopped treating the soil as a substrate for yield and started treating it as a living organism with its own intelligence. In its place came Desi cow manure, fermented bio-cultures, cover crops, and patience.

The soil did not recover overnight. In Year 1, yields dropped. In Year 2, the first signs of biological life returned. By Year 4, pesticide residue tests returned zero — and have not changed since.

The Method

What ICBN farming actually means on 28 acres.

Indigenous Cow-Based Natural farming is not a certification. It is a complete agricultural philosophy rooted in pre-Green Revolution Indian farming systems. Here is how each principle operates on our land.

Principle 01

No synthetics. Not once. Not ever.

We have never applied synthetic fertilisers, herbicides, or pesticides to our land. Not because it is inconvenient — because it is incompatible with the biology we are building. Soil fertility is constructed through biological cycles alone.

Principle 02

The Desi cow is not a symbol. She is the farm.

Our Gir and Sahiwal herd produces Jeevamrit, Panchagavya, and Gomaya compost — the three pillars of ICBN soil fertility. Each delivers hundreds of millions of beneficial microorganisms per application. No imported inputs. A closed-loop system.

Principle 03

No hybrid seeds. No proprietary genetics. No compromises.

Every seed sown here is a saved, open-pollinated heirloom variety. We maintain our own seed bank and exchange with a network of traditional farmers. When you eat ANAAD grain, you eat a variety that has no patent attached to it.

Principle 04

We feed the soil before we ask anything of it.

Between each grain crop, we sow nitrogen-fixing legumes — dhaincha, sunhemp, sesbania — and incorporate them into the soil while still green. This eliminates urea entirely and builds organic matter season after season.

Principle 05

Shallow. Single pass. Protect what's below.

Deep ploughing destroys the fungal mycelium network that forms the backbone of healthy soil. We plough shallow and once per season — protecting the biological architecture that took six years to rebuild.

Principle 06

Monsoon-fed. Microbe-retained. Chemical-free.

ICBN soil has absorbed organic matter for six consecutive years. Our water retention has improved by an estimated 40% — meaning we need less irrigation, produce less runoff, and place less pressure on the Haryana water table.

The Growing Calendar

A year on our farm. Month by month.

Nothing on this land is improvised. Every decision follows a calendar tested over six growing seasons and thousands of years of indigenous agricultural knowledge.

June – July

Green Manure Sowing

The field earns its fertility before anything is asked of it. Dhaincha and sunhemp are sown to fix atmospheric nitrogen and build organic matter through the monsoon months.

September

Incorporation & Preparation

The green crop is cut and turned into the soil while still alive. Gomaya compost follows at 4 tonnes per acre. Jeevamrit applications begin — activating the biological population ahead of sowing.

October

Heirloom Sowing

Saved seed goes into prepared soil. Traditional spacing allows light penetration and airflow. No pre-treatment. No fungicide dressing. The seed relies entirely on its own genetic vigour — and six seasons of soil biology below it.

Nov – Feb

Crop Maintenance

Four months of attention. Jeevamrit foliar sprays every 21 days. Panchagavya at tillering and grain-fill. Manual weeding in the first 30 days. No herbicides. No shortcuts.

March

Harvest

Grain is cut at 12–14% moisture. Every batch immediately receives a Batch ID. SGS India receives a sample the same week. Nothing is milled until the lab report returns clear.

April – May

Processing & Dispatch

Sun-dried on bamboo platforms. Hand-selected for seed saving. Cold stone-milled within 14 days. Dispatched to subscribers within 72 hours. The cycle closes. And begins again.
